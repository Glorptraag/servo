## DC motor · Level 1 · actuators

Record: `packages/content/parts/level-1/dc-motor.json`

- **What it does (L1):** Turns electricity into spinning.
- **Needs (L2):** Needs: power (red) in a loop, in at plus and out at minus.
- **Gives (L2):** Gives: a spinning shaft (grey).
- **Popular mechanics (L2):** A DC motor like this spins the blades of a hand-held fan.
- **Setting, unlocks at Level 2:** Direction
- **Setting, unlocks at Level 3:** Speed

| Failure mode | Card line | Hint | Teaching note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| no-circuit | No complete circuit: the shaft stays still. | This motor is not in a loop with the battery pack | Current flows only round a complete loop: out of the battery pack, through the motor and back. With one wire missing, nothing flows, so the shaft stays still. |
| low-voltage | Too little power: it turns slowly. | This motor is not getting enough power | Speed rises with voltage. On too little, such as a single cell, the motor turns slowly, and the battery pack drains more for each turn it makes. |
| overload | Too much to turn: it stops and hums. | This motor cannot turn its load | A motor can turn only so much load. Asked for more, such as up a steep ramp or against a wall, the shaft stops and the motor hums while it draws the most current it ever draws, so the battery pack drains fast. |
| reversed | Wires swapped: it turns the other way. | This motor's plus and minus are swapped | A DC motor turns one way when its plus meets plus, and the other way when its wires swap. On a robot, one motor wired the other way round spins it on the spot. |

## 2-cell battery pack · Level 1 · power

Record: `packages/content/parts/level-1/battery-pack-2-cell.json`

- **What it does (L1):** Stores electricity and gives it to the parts you wire to it.
- **Needs (L2):** Needs: power lines (red) in a loop from plus, through a part, back to minus.
- **Gives (L2):** Gives: power (red) from two AA cells.
- **Popular mechanics (L2):** The same AA cells power a TV remote and a torch.
- **Safety note:** Use real battery packs only with an adult nearby, and never join plus straight to minus.

| Failure mode | Card line | Hint | Teaching note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| short-circuit | Short circuit: the battery pack drains fast. | This battery pack is in a loop with nothing in it that uses power | When plus is joined back to minus with nothing that uses power in between, such as a bare wire, a closed switch or a different battery pack side by side, nothing holds the current back, so the pack empties in seconds and grows warm. |
| no-loop | No loop: nothing on the battery pack works. | This battery pack has no loop back to minus | A battery pack gives power only round a closed loop from plus back to minus. With no loop at all, nothing flows and nothing wired to it works. |

## servo motor · Level 2 · actuators

Record: `packages/content/parts/level-2/servo-motor.json`

- **What it does (L1):** Turns to an angle you choose, and holds it.
- **Needs (L2):** Needs: power (red) and a signal (yellow).
- **Gives (L2):** Gives: a turning arm.
- **Popular mechanics (L2):** The same part steers a radio-controlled car and moves a camera gimbal.
- **Safety note:** A real servo motor comes with small horns and screws, so build with an adult nearby.
- **Setting, unlocks at Level 3:** Angle

| Failure mode | Card line | Hint | Teaching note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| no-signal | No signal: the arm stays where it is and hums. | The servo is waiting for a signal | A servo motor turns to the angle its signal asks for. With power but no signal it has nothing to follow, so it holds where it is and hums. A microcontroller gives that signal on a signal line (yellow), and no Level 2 part gives one. |
| no-circuit | No complete circuit: the arm stays still, with no hum. | This servo motor is not in a loop with the battery pack | Without a complete loop from plus, through the servo motor and back to minus, it gets no power, so its arm stays still and it makes no sound. |
| low-voltage | Too little power: the arm turns slowly and weakly, or not at all. | This servo motor is not getting enough power | A servo motor is made for 4.8 to 6 volts. On less, such as one 2-cell battery pack, its arm turns slowly and pushes weakly, and below about 2.5 volts it does nothing at all. |
| reversed | Wires swapped: the arm stays still, with no hum. | This servo motor's plus and minus are swapped | A servo motor works only with plus to plus. Wired the other way round it does nothing at all, and a real one can be damaged this way. |
| overload | Too much to push: the arm stops and hums. | This servo motor cannot move its load | A servo motor can push or hold only so much. Asked for more, its arm stops short of its angle and hums while it draws the most current it ever draws, so the battery pack drains fast. |
