### Physics, re-traced on both kits

Same frame rules as before:
- a real motor can only be rotated, never mirrored;
- so a real motor on a mirrored mount turns the opposite way to the app's for the same wiring;
- the left mount is not mirrored, so the left motor behaves the same in the app and in a real kit.

**Rolling Start.** The printed power lines wire the real kit like this:
- battery minus (−) to the right motor's plus (+);
- switch side B to the right motor's minus (−).

So the real right motor runs on reversed polarity. Being turned round, it drives forward, matching the app. The left motor is wired plus to plus and drives forward. The real robot drives forward.

**Circuit Crew.** The real kit wires motor driver B (+) to the right motor's minus (−), the same reversal, so the robot drives forward. The switch on the mirrored right inner mount is not crossed, which is correct.

**Combined reversals.** Each reversal (mirror, Direction set to Backward, driver channel set to Backward) is an independent sign flip, and the export combines them correctly. I traced one double case by hand: driver channel B on Backward plus the mirrored right motor. Both ends of that wire are swapped, so the real wiring equals the app's. The real right motor then drives backward, as the app's does.

**Motor driver and its signal.** The export ignores a channel's setting when a signal wire reaches that channel. That matches sim-core, where a driven signal's level sets the command (`behaviour/primitives.ts:108-111`).

## Physical check of the D27 wiring advice

**Geometry** (schema `geometry/frames.ts`):
- A part frame has +x forward, +y left and +z up.
- `spin()` uses the right-hand rule about the placed axis, flipped when the placement is mirrored.
- sim-core `mechanical/robot.ts:54` takes each wheel's turning from `spin()`. The wheel rolls along (spin.y, −spin.x).
- The DC motor's shaft axis is +y, at y = +13 in the motor's own frame.
- On the chassis, `motor-left` (y = +53) is not mirrored. `motor-right` (y = −53) and `motor-right-inner` are mirrored.

**Tracing each motor:**
- **Left motor, not mirrored.** The shaft points outward to +y. At forward polarity ω = +ŷ, so the tyre's contact point moves −x and the robot moves +x. The placement is a proper rotation, which a real motor can copy, so the real motor behaves the same as the app's.
- **Right motor, mirrored, in the app.** The shaft is turned to −y (outward), and the mirror flips the sense: ω = −(−ŷ) = +ŷ. That is forward, and it matches sim-core's test that Rolling Start drives forward (`mechanical.test.ts:386`).
- **Right motor in a real kit.** A real motor can only be rotated outward (yaw 180°, or rolled 180° about x), so ω = −ŷ: wired alike, it drives backward. Crossing that motor's leads makes it match the app.
- **Conclusion.** The motor on the mirrored mount, and only that one, needs its leads crossed. The export says exactly that for both kits: "DC motor (right motor mount)". In Circuit Crew the crossing is on motor-driver channel B. The left motor is never crossed.

**Builder questions:**
- **Q1 (Backward on a mirrored mount cancels).** Correct. In the app, the right motor with the mirror and Backward turns backward. A real motor wired as the app shows also turns backward, so no crossing is needed. Backward on the unmirrored left motor does need a crossing, and the export gives one.
- **Q2 (servo motor not mentioned).** Silence is the safe choice. A mirrored servo motor's arm turns the other way in the app (`spin` of +z is −z). Reversing a servo motor's supply does not fix that and can damage it, so the export rightly never tells anyone to cross a servo motor's leads. See question 2.
- **Q4 ("large wheel 1/2").** Acceptable. Each linkage line ties the wheel to a named motor ("DC motor (left motor mount), shaft to large wheel 1, hub"), so a reader can tell the wheels apart.

**Other parts on mirrored mounts.** Circuit Crew's switch sits on the right inner motor mount and is correctly not crossed. Switches, LEDs, buzzers and bumper switches are electrically the same when mirrored. Passive drivetrain parts such as a gearbox or wheel are flipped twice in the sim, so they act as real ones do.

## Findings

1. **High · D27, rule 9 (plain, unambiguous instructions) · `src/export/parts-list.ts:35-44`, `:202`; `src/export/view.tsx:173-180`.** The "cross the leads" notes come after lines that already cross them, inside the same numbered `<ol>`. For Rolling Start the printed page reads in this order:
   - Intro: "One line for each connection, as it is made in a real kit."
   - Lines 9 and 12 already wire the right motor crossed, marked "(crossed for a real kit)".
   - Line 13: "In a real kit, cross the two leads of the DC motor (right motor mount): its plus (+) lead goes where the app shows minus (−)…"
   - Line 15: "An adult should cross the leads, with the power disconnected."

   Steps 13 and 15 read as further actions. An adult who wires lines 1–12 and then follows 13 and 15 crosses the leads a second time, and the robot spins. "Where the app shows" also points at wiring that is not on the page. The data is correct; the wording and layout turn it into a wrong instruction.

   The fix should either:
   - (a) show the connection lines as the app has them, and let the three notes be the one place the crossing happens; or
   - (b) keep the crossed lines and change the note so it explains and does not instruct, for example: "The two lines marked 'crossed for a real kit' already swap the leads of the DC motor (right motor mount). Do not swap them again." Render the notes outside the numbered list, next to the Adult supervision section.

   Either way, add a unit test that pins the wording and a browser test showing the notes sit outside the `<ol>`. See question 1.
2. **Low · rule 9 / consistency · `src/export/parts-list.ts:62-70`, `:125-135`.** The export crosses a DC motor whose own Direction setting is Backward, because a real motor has no such setting. It ignores the motor driver's Motor A / Motor B choice settings (Forward, Stop, Backward; `binds.param: 'command'`). The same reasoning applies to them: `busy-workbench` has `motor-b: backward`. A Level 2 build with a driver channel on Backward exports as if it were Forward, so the real robot does not match the app. No kit fixture is affected, because both use the defaults. See question 3.
3. **Low · latent correctness · `src/export/parts-list.ts:92-101`, `:132`.** Mirroring is read from the direct mount point's `mirrored` flag. Using `placeParts(blueprint, catalogue).get(id).placement.mirrored` would stay correct if a mounted host is ever added. It is not blocking.
4. **Low · copy pass · `src/export/parts-list.ts:162`, `:166`, `:172`.** The wiring line templates are inline literals, not entries in `EXPORT_TEXT` or `CROSSING_TEXT`: "Mount", the "(colour): … to …" frame, and ", ". The browser test proves this with emulated print media.
