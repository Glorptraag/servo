2. **minor** · test reliability · packages/canvas/test/wiring/rules.test.ts:31 and :93
   - Two O(sockets²) sweeps run under Vitest's default 5 s timeout. They take 1.8 to 7.9 s on this loaded machine and failed three times running at the default, including two retries.
   - The sweep at :72 already has 60 s.
   - CI on a slower runner could flake the same way. Fix: give both tests an explicit timeout.
3. **minor** · brief Sections 9 ("ports are at least 44 px touch targets") and 11 ("empty vs connected is readable at a glance"), D65 · packages/canvas/docs/wiring.md:62
   - At rest, crowded sockets still overlap. On the Circuit Crew kit robot some sit 5.6 px apart, and a covered socket shows as a sliver.
   - Whether a socket is empty or filled shows only once its crowd fans out.
   - D65 recommends that "task 3.3 fixes the socket overlap before G3". Fanning out on demand does make every socket reachable and unambiguous, which is the part that matters for input. It does not fix how the robot looks at rest (Question 1).
4. **minor** · D44 · packages/canvas/src/wiring/controller.ts:903-905, crowds.ts:248
   - The reach is 32 px divided by the drag sensitivity, and the smallest fan circle is the reach plus a quarter socket.
   - So at a low sensitivity the fan throws sockets far out. My pure probe, at zoom 1:

     | Sensitivity | Fanned sockets sit from the press |
     | --- | --- |
     | 1 | 55–88 px |
     | 0.25 | 139–172 px |
     | 0.1 | about 330 px |

   - While a wire waits, any press within that reach of any socket is taken as the wire's second tap. At low sensitivities that leaves little bench near the build for panning.
   - The interface fixes no range for `dragSensitivity` ("Positive; 1 is the default") (Question 2).
5. **minor** · 3.2's rule that what shows on top is what a press reaches · controller.ts:890-892, renderer/surface.ts:281-294
   - `beginPlacement` and `cancelPlacement` do not tell wiring. A waiting wire's ring and glows, an open fan, or a wire's bin stay drawn while a tray part waits for its tap.
   - Meanwhile wiring defers every press, so a tap on the visible bin or a fanned socket places the part there instead.
   - A placement that lands changes the build and clears these marks. A cancelled one brings the waiting wire back.
   - Found by reading the code; I did not probe it with real input. Fix: let the wire go, and close the fan and the bin, when a placement begins.
6. **minor** · brief Section 11 ("UI motion is short (120–200 ms)") · controller.ts:731-733
   - When a tapped socket refuses a waiting wire, the wire reaches for it and springs back as one 280 ms motion (120 + 160). Each half is within range, but the whole is not.
   - `motion.test.ts` checks only the separate constants. A shorter reach, or 200 ms in total, would keep inside the brief.
