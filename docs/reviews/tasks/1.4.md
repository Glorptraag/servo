### Findings

1. major · done-when (D61), rule 9 · src/mechanical/drive.ts:136-141 · Sliding friction on a frame point is made viscous with the coefficient μN ÷ max(speed at the start of the substep, 1 mm/s). So while the robot speeds up, the friction is μN × (new speed ÷ old speed).
   - On `broken-chassis-on-the-floor`'s first substep it is 1.62 N against μN = 0.73 N.
   - At the end of tick 1 the robot is at 17.8 mm/s. A fine-step integration of the same model gives 70.4 mm/s, gripping from about 12 ms.
   - So the wheels read `slipping` for tick 1, at every pack voltage from 2.2 to 3.0 V. The fixture shows `chassis: scraping` plus `wheel-left: slipping` and `wheel-right: slipping`. Its `expect` is exactly `chassis: scraping`, and D61 makes this fixture 1.4's balance acceptance.
   - The same happens to the geared robot with a loose caster (`caster: loose` plus both `slipping`, against the ruling of exactly one fault) and to the small-wheel robot with a loose caster (ticks 1–2).
   - It contradicts mechanical.md:215 ("a slip that ends within a tick shows nothing").
   - Capping the friction at μN, with one corrective pass in a scratch copy, removed every extra fault and raised tick 1's speed to 48 mm/s.
2. major · brief §4 (props to push), D52 · src/mechanical/world.ts:141-158, tick.ts:173-175 · A free prop's floor friction is applied only to its velocity after `world.step()`. The step itself just shares momentum between the robot and the prop, so friction never resists the push.
   - The direct-drive robot can push at most 1.12 N, which moves a box of about 143 g. It pushes 300 g at 24.7 mm/s, 700 g at 10.7 mm/s and 2 kg at 3.4 mm/s, and its motors are never held.
   - The geared robot "slips" against 200 g or more, while still moving 2 kg. Physically it grips and pushes 200 g at about 44 mm/s, and moves nothing above about 270 g.
   - The 50 g content box behaves correctly. D52's default gearbox lesson ("the bare motor stalls or slips against it and the geared robot pushes it") cannot be built, and R-2.4 left 1.4 to confirm those numbers.
   - mechanical.md:105 should also say how a heavy prop resists.
