### Builder decisions

1. **DC motor at 0.12 kg·cm at 6 V (D45): not accepted as the default.**
   - Rule 13 asks for the most conservative reading, and the brief states 0.4 kg·cm at 6 V. The weaker figure exists only so that the bare robot stalls on task 2.4's 1-in-7 hill. The brief's figure still meets the other tuning goals: 5.0 s across the floor, and 0.48 of that speed on one cell.
   - **It reverses D33's queued default.** Under D33, the Level 1 robot climbs the gentle hill, and task 4.8 teaches the gearbox on a steeper preset or a heavier load. D45 does not mention D33.
   - **It takes wheel slip out of Levels 1–2.** Neither the wheel's `slipping` mode nor the brief's "wheels squeal on a fast start" (Section 4) can happen on the reference robot, even through the gearbox. With the brief's figure, a gearbox build slips and squeals.
   - **It weakens the overload note.** The note's "steep ramp" becomes an 8° hill.
   - **D45's description is wrong.** D45 calls 0.12 kg·cm "a realistic small hobby motor". The part drawn, 64 × 22 × 20 mm in yellow and silver, is a TT-type gear motor rated 0.8 kg·cm at 6 V and 200 rpm (see Sources). 0.12 kg·cm is a bare motor's figure, and a bare motor turns thousands of rpm, not 300.
2. **The LED has a mount port: accept.** "Drive forward and light the LED" (brief Section 5) needs the LED to ride on the robot, and under D19 an unmounted part stays where it was put. The port adds no need, so an LED left on the bench still lights.
3. **Motors stall before wheels slip against a wall: accept the physics.** This holds with either motor figure in direct drive, so the sentence in `packages/schema/docs/documents.md:73` is wrong for Level 1, whatever D45 decides. The cross-and-stop goal is still right, because it requires that no DC motor is powered (finding 7).
4. **Overload also shows drain: accept.** Stall current is the most the motor ever draws, here about 2.8× its running current. A behaviour fixture should assert the drain per second, because D17's per-turn reading means nothing at zero speed.
