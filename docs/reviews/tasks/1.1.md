**`wiredNeeds` on legal-but-wrong builds.** Every case builds without error, and the graph is consistent with the schema in each:
- **Reversed motor (fixture).** No wiring need is unmet. With the switch closed, motor-right's use runs from the battery's − node to its + node, so the solver will see it reversed. Pushes times the wiring sign give a spin.
- **Short circuit (fixture).** The pack's two ends sit on one node and its net is live. `wiredNeeds` shows the pack shorted.
- **Switch across the pack.** Closed, the pack's ends share a node, and `wiredNeeds` shows the pack and the switch shorted. Open, nothing is live. With a motor beside it, the motor's ends share a live node, and the motor is open, explained by the short.
- **Servo motor with power and no signal.** Its supply nets are live and its signal in is joined to nothing. `wiredNeeds` shows nothing unmet, because the signal need is sim-core's to judge.
- **Equal packs in parallel**, with a motor and alone: both packs give power and the nets are live. Nothing is unmet.
- **Unequal packs in parallel.** The graph is the same as for equal packs, and `wiredNeeds` shows both packs shorted. The graph leaves this short to the solver, which has the volts, as graph.md says. It shows only a pack whose two ends share a node.
- **Motor wired between two working LED loops.** All four nets are live, including both of the motor's ends, and the motor is open. This is the documented gap between live and powered.
- **LED bypassed by a wire.** Its ends sit on one live node, and the LED is open.
- **Motor driver.**
  - Without power: nothing is live. The driver's power need is unmet with no explanation, so it shows as a fault, and the motor is explained by its feeder.
  - Powered, with channel a at stop: channel a gives no power, the motor's nets are dead, and the motor is explained by the controls.
  - With an output wired back into its own supply: it cannot power itself, as in `wiredNeeds`.
- **Motor off the 3V pin (fixture).** The rail gives power, the motor's nets are live, and nothing is unmet. The weak pin is the solver's lesson.

**Independent oracle.** I wrote my own oracle from first principles:
- nodes by a plain search over closed switches;
- outputs giving power by a simple-path search, through a source giving power, outside the output's own part, grown until nothing changes;
- a net live when its node lies on a simple cycle through a source giving power;
- a use powered when a simple cycle runs through it and a source giving power.

I ran it on 2,500 random circuits of up to 7 parts: two packs, switches, bumper switches, LEDs, motors, buzzers, servo motors, motor drivers and microcontrollers. Each circuit ran at rest and at two random states, with odd values included: a switch given as 1, and channels at NaN, −0 and 0.3. That is 7,500 states:
- Nodes, sources giving power and live nets had 0 mismatches.
- For every single-use part, `wiredNeeds`' power verdict equals my "powered".
- Every powered use has both ends live.
- In 16 states, a use had both ends live but no power: the bridge case.

The builder's own property test does not check `nodes` independently (finding 3). Mine does.

**Mirrored mounts and drive pushes.** I checked the 8 builds plus two variants: bumper-robot with gear-right loose, and rolling-start with both motors crossed.
- `pushes` equals `drivePushes`, and every placement equals `placeParts`.
- Every mount's `local` equals `mountPlacement`, and its `mirrored` is the mount point's flag. The right-hand motor and gearbox mounts are mirrored (D23).
- Every drive's `carried` equals `carriedPlacement` and matches the carried part's placement.
- Pushes times the wiring sign at rest give: rolling-start [1, 1] (forward), reversed-motor [1, −1] (spin), and both motors crossed [−1, −1] (backward).
- With gear-right loose, the right wheel gets no push.
