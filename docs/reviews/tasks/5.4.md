## The builder's decisions

1. **Deck from all 14 Level 1–2 records rather than "kit parts".** **Accept.** Brief Section 14 says "name 8 of 10 kit parts". The Level 2 kit, `kits/circuit-crew.json`, holds all 14 Level 1–2 parts, and the Level 1 kit holds a subset of them. So "Level 1–2 parts" and "the kit parts at the end of Level 2" are the same set today. Filtering on `identity.level` follows ground rule 1 and D40's "ten cards drawn from Level 1–2 parts" (packages/app/docs/store.md:16). The two would only diverge if a later Level 1–2 part were left out of both kits (see Q1).
2. **No timer.** **Accept.** "Two-minute" describes how long a round takes. A countdown would put pressure on a 6-year-old and is close to the streak and lives mechanics rule 7 excludes. This is the conservative choice.
3. **Name hidden until "Show the name".** **Accept.** This is conservative: a child who can read cannot read the answer off the screen. It is a native disclosure button with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` (`view.tsx:233-238`), and the browser test covers it.
4. **A stopped, interrupted or hidden round keeps nothing.** **Accept.** A partial round is not the 10-card check, and keeping nothing is the data-minimal choice. Stop and a child switch are tested. "Page hidden" works because the 5.1 gate closes the view on `visibilitychange` (`src/accounts/view.tsx:75-82`), which unmounts the section. That path has no card-game test (F4).
5. **Summary "n of 10 parts named." on the same screen.** **Reject.** See F1.

## Findings

**F1 · major · rule 7, rule 13, D40 · `src/card-game/view.tsx:177-195`, `:30`, `:18`, `:96-97`**
After the tenth mark, the view stores the round and immediately shows "This round", "n of 10 parts named." and a per-part named/not-named list, with focus moved to the summary. This is the one screen in the parent view that the child is invited to watch, so a child who can read sees a score the moment the round ends.

This conflicts with several existing statements:
- D40 as recorded: "The child never sees a score" (packages/app/docs/store.md:16).
- The package's own pre-existing README line: "the child never sees a score".
- The section's own intro: "The child sees only the pictures, never a result" (`view.tsx:18`). That text is now untrue.
- Rule 13, which says to pick the most conservative interpretation and flag it. The builder flagged the question (README decision 5) but shipped the less conservative option.

The progress section (5.2) showing the same count was accepted because it sits behind the gate for the adult to read alone. The card game is different, because the child is present by design.

A conservative design would do this:
- After the tenth mark, keep the round and show one neutral line, such as "The round is kept." This gives no count and no per-part list.
- Offer a separate adult control, such as "Show the result" (a disclosure button with `aria-expanded`, like Show the name). Focus moves to that control, not to the result.
- Only that press reveals the count and the per-part list. The intro can then truthfully say the child never sees a result unless the adult chooses to show it.
- Optionally, leave the count out of the game section entirely and say "Progress shows the latest round", since the progress section already shows it.

The browser test should assert that no "of 10 parts named" text exists in the card-game section until the adult presses the control.

**F2 · minor · rule 8 · `src/card-game/view.tsx:95-99`, `:166-175`**
When the last mark is pressed, the Card unmounts and focus falls to `body` during `saving`. On `failed`, focus is never placed, so keyboard and screen-reader users lose their place exactly when "The round could not be kept" is announced. Move focus to "Try keeping it again" on `failed`.

**F3 · minor · data loss · `src/card-game/view.tsx:171`**
In the `failed` state, "Play another round" throws away a complete, unsaved 10-card round with no confirmation. Either drop that button from the failed state, or say inline that the unkept round will be lost.

**F4 · minor · tests · `test/browser/card-game.test.tsx`**
The README says a hidden page keeps nothing, but no card-game test covers that path. Add one case: start a round, mark a card, dispatch `visibilitychange` with the page hidden, reopen through the gate, and assert there are no rounds.

**F5 · note · `src/card-game/view.tsx:132`**
`canPlay` reshuffles the whole deck on every render just to test for emptiness. `deckParts(content).length > 0` says what is meant.
