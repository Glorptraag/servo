## The five checks asked for

1. **Tidy reaches `applyEdit` as an EditCommand.** `tidyWires()` calls `apply({ kind: 'tidy-wires' })` (surface.ts:344-346). `apply` runs `applyEdit` (surface.ts:280), whose table now has `ROUTING_REDUCERS` (placement/apply.ts:17), then routes the wires (surface.ts:286). The list view's `TIDY_WIRES_ACTION` carries the same command (routing/commands.ts:13-17), and the browser test shows `tidyWires()` and `apply(TIDY_WIRES_ACTION.does.command)` give identical routes and no `edit` event. What is missing:
   - **Touch and pointer.** By ruling, the app owns the button. The app's `ZoomControl` (packages/app/src/shell/zoom-control.tsx) has zoom in, Fit and zoom out, and no tidy button. No plan task names one.
   - **List view.** Task 3.6 has not landed, so `TIDY_WIRES_ACTION` is offered by nothing yet. `ListModel.perform` says it "Returns false when it changed nothing" (interface.ts:474), and tidying never changes the blueprint, so 3.6 must count changed routes as a change.
2. **Interface changes.**
   - `CanvasSafeArea`, `TidyWires` and the fuller doc comments are additive for callers. They are documented in the README, docs/commands.md, docs/renderer.md and the new docs/routing.md.
   - `setSafeArea` is a new required member of an interface the app implements in a test stand-in, so it breaks the app's typecheck (finding 1).
   - `TidyWires` joins the `SingleEdit` union. That is fine today because nothing outside canvas switches on it exhaustively.
3. **Is the "never crosses" test independent of the router's maths? Yes, for the definition it tests.**
   - router.test.ts:29-49 samples each path every 0.08 mm with its own `insideBody`, built on the scene's `canvasToPart` and `record.body.size`. It never uses `shapes.ts` or `segmentEnters`.
   - The flood fill (router.test.ts:57-120) is also independent.
   - The test's body is the full `body.size`, where the router uses `min(body.size, tile)`, so the test is the stricter of the two.
   - What it cannot catch is a wrong definition of a body: it shares the router's (finding 2).
   - The camera's "findable" check, by contrast, uses the same `part.bounds` targets as the implementation (camera.test.ts:146-154), so it cannot catch finding 3.
4. **Do the zoom limits hold at minimum zoom, maximum zoom and after pan?**
   - Zoom is clamped to [min(1, fit)/2, 4] in the uncovered view (camera.ts:129-139).
   - Every pan and zoom is held by `holdFocus` (view.ts:96-104), which slides the view along the limits and never jumps.
   - The unit and browser tests cover 400%, the minimum, pans of 1e6 px, panels, and 2000 mixed gestures.
   - The weakness is the target: a part's axis-aligned bounds, including socket reach (scene.ts:148), not its drawn tile or sockets (finding 3).
   - `resize` and `setSafeArea` do not re-hold the view. After either, a pan may "keep its distance" outside the limits (view.ts:96-104). This is documented, and it relies on the app calling `fit` (finding 9).
5. **Why the count fell from 225 to 219.** fd80d98 removed six tests from router.test.ts. Statically, 11 `it(` became 9, but `describe.each` multiplies by 5 builds, so 27 runtime tests became 21:
   - **The "busy workbench" block went from 4 tests to 3 (−1).** It removed three tests:
     - "no wire crosses a part body but the three whose socket is boxed in";
     - "boxes in the motor driver plus (+) and the LED plus (+) and minus (-)";
     - "routes a boxed-in wire clean but for one straight run out".
   - It added two:
     - "none of its 43 wires crosses a part body";
     - "has open workbench between the two sockets of every wire".
   - **"leaves each socket away from its own part, never back across it" × 5 builds was deleted (−5).**
   - **Why.** Once bodies shrank from tiles to `body.size`, no busy-workbench socket is boxed in, so the boxed-in tests no longer applied. The "away from its own part" property now fails on two wires: `w23` at `servo.plus` on the bumper robot and `w35` at `br-servo.plus` on the 25-part fixture.
   - **Effect.** Their last 7.9 mm segment comes in from behind the socket, but it lies inside the socket's 8.8 mm disc, so it is hidden. The removal is defensible, but nothing says why (finding 6).
   - commands.test.ts changed one test body and no count.

## Findings

1. **BLOCKER (rules 6 and 14). The new required `setSafeArea` breaks the app's typecheck, so `pnpm check` and CI fail.**
   - Where: packages/canvas/src/interface.ts:181; packages/app/test/browser/shell.test.tsx:34 (`class StandInCanvas implements CanvasHandle`) and :167.
   - Why: adding a required member is additive for callers but not for implementers.
   - Fix: give the stand-in a `setSafeArea`, or make the member optional, and note the change. 3.7's file scope does not reach `packages/app`, so the orchestrator should allow the one-line stand-in edit or route it to the app.
2. **MAJOR (done-when; brief Section 9). The router's "body" is not the drawn part.**
   - **What the router uses.** `bodyShape` uses `body.size` at true size (packages/canvas/src/routing/shapes.ts:51-62). The doc comments say this is "the part as drawn" (router.ts:9-11, docs/routing.md:27).
   - **What the renderer draws.** A filled, edged card the size of the whole tile (src/renderer/views.ts:104-112). It scales the picture to fill the tile less 2 mm of padding (views.ts:179-189). The placeholder art fills the body box in true proportions, so a small part's picture is drawn well beyond `body.size`. On these fixtures the factor is: LED 4.3–5.7, buzzer 2.9, servo motor 1.8, motor driver 1.7, caster 1.6, switch 1.3.
   - **What the child sees.** Treating the picture as `body.size` scaled the way the renderer scales it, routed lines run over another part's drawn picture:

     | Build | Lines over another part's picture |
     | --- | --- |
     | Busy workbench | 11 of 32 (w12 and w15 over the caster, w13 and w14 over the LED, w20 and w21 over the motor driver, …) |
     | 25-part fixture | 8 |
     | Bumper robot | 5 |
     | Circuit Crew | 8 |
     | Rolling Start | 3 (w8, w11 and w12 over the caster) |

   - **Against the tile cards.** Routed lines cross another part's drawn tile card in 10 of 17 routed busy-workbench wires.
   - **Why the tests miss it.** They share the router's definition, so they cannot see this.
   - **The ruling.** D80 says "a part body is the drawn outline, not the 96 px touch tile", but in this renderer the drawn outline is the scaled picture on its card. The ruling and the renderer disagree, and the builder resolved it toward the data rather than the screen. Needs a ruling (Q1).
3. **MAJOR (done-when; brief Section 10). The zoom limits can lose the build: they keep 96 px of each part's axis-aligned bounds, not 96 px of anything drawn.**
   - Where: `targets()` uses `part.bounds` (src/renderer/surface.ts:442-447). Those bounds are the tile plus every socket's reach (src/scene/scene.ts:148), so they have empty margins wherever one socket sticks out, and empty corners when a part is turned.
   - Repro, in Node against the branch's own `Camera`, `limitsFor` and `buildScene`, with the 1180 × 820 iPad canvas and panels `{ top: 64, right: 340, bottom: 96, left: 0 }`:
     1. Place one chassis at the origin, upright.
     2. Fit, then `zoomAbout` to 4.
     3. `panBy(-1e6, 1e6)`.
   - Result:
     - The uncovered view shows plane x 80.6 to 164.6 and y −121.4 to −55.4.
     - The chassis tile ends at x = 80.
     - The nearest socket is at (80, 0), 55 mm away.
     - Nothing drawn is in view.
   - Other cases from the same probe:
     - A chassis at 45 degrees shows nothing in all four diagonal corners, even at zoom 1.
     - A battery pack or LED at 45 degrees shows nothing at 400%.
     - Rotations are any value in [0, 360) in the schema and in `rotate-part`, though D69 snaps drags to quarter turns.
   - In the five fixtures, some drawn pixels always stayed in view, but often less than a 96 × 96 block.
   - The tests use the same bounds (camera.test.ts:146-154), so they pass.
   - A first chassis on an empty workbench, zoomed in and dragged, is the likeliest way for a Level 1 child to hit this.
4. **MAJOR (rule 8). Tidying has no touch or pointer path yet, and no task owns one.**
   - The ruling gives the button to the app. The merged 4.1 `ZoomControl` has none (packages/app/src/shell/zoom-control.tsx:14-31).
   - `App.tsx` passes no `onSafeArea` that forwards to `setSafeArea`, and nothing calls `fit` after the first load, which ruling D80 also gives to the app.
   - The list view's action exists only as a constant until 3.6 lands.
   - So today a child can tidy by no path at all. Rule 8 asks for all three paths "delivered together, not later". See Q3.
5. **MINOR (rule 14, tests). One test's name promises more than it checks.**
   - Where: router.test.ts:166-176. The test is "routes the wires that crossed a body, from socket to socket, and leaves the rest straight", but it no longer asserts the last clause. It was relaxed from an equality to one direction, because the sampler can miss a graze.
   - The behaviour holds: my 0.01 mm probe found no routed wire whose straight line was clean, on all five builds.
   - Fix: assert the other direction with the router's exact `crossesBodies` and the sampler together, or rename the test.
6. **MINOR (rule 14, docs). The removed tests are not explained, and some code paths are now untested.**
   - "Leaves each socket away from its own part" was deleted without a note.
   - docs/routing.md:29 and router.ts:6 still say a route "leaves its socket straight out", which is false for w23 and w35 (hidden under the socket, see check 5).
   - The boxed-in path now has no test, because no fixture boxes a socket. That path is `exitsOf` with `crossing > 0`, `cleanBeyondExits` and `ESCAPE_MM` (router.ts:193-233, 329-335).
   - Fix: a small synthetic build with a boxed-in socket would keep that path tested.
7. **MINOR (rule 14). The fixture copy has no drift check.**
   - test/fixtures/busy-workbench.json is today an exact copy of `packages/content/fixtures/blueprints/busy-workbench.json`, its 10 part records (packages/content/parts/…) and `arenas/open-floor.json`. I compared each.
   - Nothing fails if content changes.
   - Fix: a check in packages/tools, which may import both, would keep it honest.
8. **MINOR (performance). Tidying runs synchronously and took longer here than the docs say.**
   - `routeWires` took about 1.0 s on the busy workbench and 0.27 s on the 25-part fixture in my run, at a load average of about 300. docs/routing.md:32 says 0.06–0.3 s.
   - It runs synchronously in `apply`. It has not been measured on the 2020 iPad.
   - This does not block, but it should be measured before G3.
9. **MINOR (brief Section 10). A resize or a new safe area does not re-hold the view.**
   - `resize` and `setSafeArea` move nothing, so a rotation of the iPad, or a panel sliding in, can leave the build covered or off screen.
   - Pans then "keep their distance" rather than pull back (view.ts:96-104).
   - This is documented, and Fit recovers, but it depends on the app wiring of finding 4.
10. **NIT. One test passes only because a later test re-enables tidying.** routing.test.ts:97-104 leaves the canvas in Run mode, and the next test's `reset` and `loadFresh` recover it. This works, but it depends on test order.

What is right and worth keeping:
- The command is the single path to tidying.
- Routes are view state, kept while their sockets stay put and dropped otherwise, as D80 says.
- The router is deterministic and does not depend on the order the blueprint lists things in.
- Hit testing follows a route.
- Dashes run round the bends.
- The safe-area maths is guarded.
- The docs are thorough.

## Questions for Drew

1. **What is a part body for tidy wires?** D80 calls it "the drawn outline". The renderer draws a 96 px tile card with the picture scaled up to fill it, while the router uses `body.size` at true size.
   - **Default:** route around the picture as the renderer draws it: the body box scaled as the renderer scales it, which is about the tile less its 2 mm padding for small parts.
   - **Alternative:** the renderer draws pictures at true size inside the touch tile. That is a 3.1 change, after which the router is right as built.
2. **What does "the build is lost" mean for the zoom limits?**
   - **Default:** less than 96 px (or all of a smaller one) of any part's drawn tile, picture or sockets is in the uncovered view, measured on the turned shape, not its bounding box. The limit and its test should both use that.
3. **Who builds the tidy button, the `onSafeArea` → `setSafeArea` wiring, and `fit` after the first load?** D80 gives them to the app, but no app task names them.
   - **Default:** the orchestrator queues a small app follow-up before G3: a tidy button beside Fit in `ZoomControl` calling `tidyWires()`, `onSafeArea` passed on to `setSafeArea`, and `fit()` after the first load. 3.6 offers `TIDY_WIRES_ACTION` and treats changed routes as a change in `perform`. 3.8's parity check covers all three paths.
4. **What should happen to a tidied route when a part moves?** Brief Section 10's table says that on move or rotate "Wires follow and re-route". D80 instead drops a route to a straight line when its socket moves.
   - **Default:** keep D80 for launch: drop to straight, and the child tidies again. Re-routing the moved part's wires automatically on drop is a later option, once the router has been timed on an iPad.
